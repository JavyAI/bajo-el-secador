डीलक्स सैलून  ·  Salón D'Lujo
Act I

The Source Grammar

What actually makes the Deluxe Saloon image work. Eight laws — everything else is negotiable vocabulary.

The eight laws

01Flat shapes, hard edges

Every form is an opaque color block. No outlines, no gradients inside a shape, no airbrush. Edges do all the drawing.

02Faceless figures

No eyes, no mouths. Identity lives in posture, clothing silhouette, skin tone, hair and mustache shapes. This is what keeps twelve people from becoming noise.

03Stage-set camera

Frontal orthographic elevation at eye level. Zero vanishing-point convergence — depth comes only from overlap and scale, like a theatre flat.

04One diagonal beam

A single hard shaft of sunlight, upper-left to lower-right, isolates the focal action from the clutter. It is the composition's only special effect.

05Uniform grain pass

Monochrome film grain over the entire image as a final layer — never per-shape. It fuses the flat blocks into one printed object (riso / faded poster feel).

06Horizontal banding

Road · sidewalk · shop (≈60% of height) · stacked hillside houses · sky strip. The scene is layered like sediment, never diagonal terrain.

07Off-center focal action

The barber and chair sit at ~55% across, lower-middle band. Balance comes from mass: heavy awning top-left vs. dark palm cluster right.

08Warm light, cool shade

Sunlit surfaces go warm ochre; shadows go deep, cool, and desaturated. Shadow color is the palette's real mood-setter.

Salón D'Lujo — La Versión Dominicana

Recortes · Afeitado · Cerquillo — hand-painted on turquoise, coral drop-shadow, a script "D'" because every Dominican shop name has one. The sign is the logo, the logo is the sign.

Scene bible

AEl local

Single-story concrete colmado-barbería painted turquesa, coral pilasters, ornamental iron rejas on the window, corrugated zinc awning on wood struts, rust bleeding at the seams. Paint peels to grey block underneath.

BLa acción

Barber in a fitted tank top and towel over the shoulder, clippers up, carving a fade. Client caped in cream, chancletas on the footrest. Same red hydraulic chair, same 55%/62% position, same beam.

CEl dominó

Right third: four men around a white plastic table mid-slam, one standing kibitzer. Crates of green bottles stacked behind them. Replaces the sack-sitters, same visual weight.

DLa calle

Painted curb, cracked concrete sidewalk, one motoconcho leaning on its stand at frame right, bocina on a stool pushing bachata into the street, viralata asleep where beam meets shade.

EEl fondo

Barrio climbing the hill: mint, rosa, celeste, lime boxes with zinc roofs, grey tinacos, laundry lines, antennas. Royal palms punctuate; one flamboyán in bloom carries coral into the sky band. Far right, between houses: a thin wedge of Caribbean sea.

Same 45° beam, slightly warmer and brighter — Caribbean sun. Warm cream beam on cool turquoise wall is a complementary pop the original never had. Shadows drop into teal-black, never brown.

Generation prompt — Salón D'Lujo

Flat vector-style digital illustration of a Dominican barrio corner barbershop
("Salón D'Lujo"), mid-morning Caribbean light, panoramic 16:9 landscape, 2560×1435.

STYLE: identical grammar to source — hard-edged flat color blocks, no outlines,
no gradients within shapes, faceless figures (identity via posture, clothing,
hair; mustaches allowed), cel lighting with ONE hard diagonal sunbeam upper-left
to lower-right, uniform film-grain/riso texture over the entire image, muted
print feel like a faded hand-painted colmado sign.

CAMERA: straight-on frontal elevation, eye level, zero perspective convergence,
stage-set facade, across-the-street distance, figures ~1/3 frame height.

BANDS bottom-to-top: dark asphalt road · painted-curb concrete sidewalk ·
single-story shop occupying middle 60% · stacked pastel barrio houses climbing
a hill · strip of cobalt sky with a thin wedge of turquoise Caribbean sea
between rooftops at far right.

HERO BUILDING: turquoise-painted concrete colmado-barbería with coral trim,
ornamental iron window grilles, corrugated zinc awning roof on wooden struts
with rust at the seams, peeling paint revealing grey block. Hand-painted sign
"SALÓN D'LUJO" in cream block letters with mango-yellow drop shadow and a
script flourish on the D'.

FOCAL (55% across, lower-middle): barber in fitted tank top, towel on shoulder,
clippers raised, cutting a fade; client in a red hydraulic barber chair draped
in a flowing cream cape, sandals on the footrest — both inside the sunbeam.

LEFT THIRD: mango-yellow canvas awning sloping from top-left; fruit vendor at
a wooden table stacked with plátanos, mangos, chinolas; customer on a stool.

RIGHT THIRD: four men playing dominoes around a white plastic table, one
mid-slam, one standing watching; stacked crates of green bottles; a large
speaker (bocina) on a stool by the door; a parked motoconcho at frame edge;
jute sacks; a street dog asleep at the edge of the light beam. Royal palm
fronds and a flamboyán tree in full red-orange bloom overhang from the right.

BACKGROUND: tiered hillside of flat-roofed houses in mint, pink, sky blue and
lime with zinc roofs, grey rooftop water tanks, laundry lines, TV antennas;
two ciguas palmeras perched on a power line above the shop.

LIGHT: high morning sun upper-left, ~45° warm cream beam raking the turquoise
wall; long shadows falling right; ALL shadows cool teal-black (#14211E), never
brown; strong warm/cool contrast between sunlit ochre concrete and shaded street.

PALETTE: turquoise #1B9E96, coral #E8503A, mango #F2A21B, barrio pink #F0A8A0,
palm green #3E6B34, Caribbean sea/sky #2277B9, zinc grey #9AA3A8, cream #F2E6CC,
teal-black shadow #14211E.

MOOD: unhurried barrio morning, bachata in the air, sun-drenched stillness,
nostalgic warmth — lofi-radio ambience.

NO outlines, NO faces with eyes/mouths, NO photorealism, NO neon saturation,
NO resort/tourist props (no cocktails, no beach chairs, no tourists) — this is
everyday barrio life, only crisp flat shapes + uniform grain.
Act III

Art-Direction Review

Six checks the Dominican version must pass before it ships. Where it stands today, on paper.

The saturation cage

"Tropical" tempts you toward neon; the style dies there. Every hue above keeps source-level chroma, and the grain pass pulls it further print-ward. Test: desaturate the finished piece — if the value structure collapses, the colors were doing work hue should never do.

Pass — palette pre-muted
The cliché filter

No cocktails, no resort, no tourists, no flag-painted walls. Dominican-ness is carried by dominó, rolos, motoconcho, colmado crates, tinacos, the cigua — things a Dominican recognizes from Tuesday, not from a brochure.

Pass — barrio, not brochure
Continuity threads

The red chair, the 45° beam, the faceless cast, the two birds, the off-center focal — five inherited elements make the pair read as siblings at a glance. Remove any two and the kinship breaks.

Pass — 5/5 threads held
Beam on turquoise

The original ran warm-on-warm (cream beam, red wall). Warm beam on turquoise is complementary — stronger, but riskier: if the beam goes yellow it turns green where it overlaps the wall. Keep the beam cream (#F2E6CC), never pure yellow.

Watch in production
Detail-density parity

Same clutter budget as the source: ~12 figures, one verb each, comparable prop count. The dominó table plus bocina plus dog is already rich — resist adding a second vendor or a guagua.

Hold the line at 12
Lettering vernacular

Dominican sign painting mixes heavy block caps, a script flourish, and hard drop shadows. "SALÓN D'LUJO" needs the accent on the Ó and the apostrophe in D' — misspelling the vernacular is the fastest way to read as outsider work.

Pass — diacritics specced
