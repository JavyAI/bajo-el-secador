## 1. Colmado / esquina radio = salsa + merengue de fiesta

**Lock text:** Esquina public = “colmado / sidewalk speaker.” AYER IN = salsa dura + merengue de fiesta. Temperature = shout / tumbao / tambora. Típica as the spine is OUT (that is Silla).

### What official sources support

Merengue is the national everyday music, not a salon specialty.

- UNESCO inscribed “Music and dance of the merengue in the Dominican Republic” in 2016. The listing says merengue “plays an active role in various aspects of people’s daily lives – from their education to social gatherings and celebrations,” and tags **everyday life, social gathering, urban spaces**.  
  https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/music-and-dance-of-the-merengue-in-the-dominican-republic-01162

- UNESCO video page for the same element.  
  https://ich.unesco.org/en/video/38864

- Dominican Music USA genre guide (cites Austerlitz): merengue as national dance music, *merengue con mambo* as the 80s–90s fiesta form the lock means by “merengue de fiesta.”  
  https://dominicanmusicusa.com/genres/merengue/35

- Austerlitz book record: *Merengue: Dominican Music and Dominican Identity* (Temple, 1997). Older than two years — still the standard academic source, not a current street survey.  
  https://archive.org/details/merenguedominica0000aust  
  Author page: http://www.paulausterlitz.org/Merengue.shtml  
  Afropop interview: https://www.afropop.org/articles/10658

- World Music Central: merengue = national music/dance; UNESCO 2016; commercial salsa also gets “wider exposure” in the DR.  
  https://worldmusiccentral.org/world-music-resources/musician-biographies/dominican-music

The colmado is a sidewalk speaker, not a quiet shop.

- Travel ethnography (2026): “A colmado – the small neighborhood store found on practically every block – almost always has speakers angled toward the street. Not because there’s a party. Just because that’s how a colmado operates.”  
  https://knycxjourneying.com/dominican-rhythms-how-merengue-and-bachata-define-a-nation/

- Las Terrenas lifestyle piece: “a tiny colmado in El Limón blasting Merengue.” Tertiary (real-estate blog), useful only as a contemporary usage of the same image.  
  https://realestatelasterrenas.com/republic-dominican-music

- Field clip: couples dancing merengue to Joan Soriano *María Elena* inside a Santo Domingo colmado.  
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3agaGDmclns

Salsa is also a colmado / barrio language, including Dominican salsa.

- Cuco Valoy, Santo Domingo, “El Brujo de la Salsa”; *Juliana* is the lock’s Dominican salsa receipt.  
  https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuco_Valoy

- Chiquito Timbal interview hook: “DEL COLMADO DE ÑOÑA AL MUNDO” — the HOY house-band founder is narrated as leaving a colmado.  
  https://www.instagram.com/reel/DJUUvwqN2Yg/?hl=en

- Colmado Ñoña event copy: live merengue at the same colmado.  
  https://www.instagram.com/reel/DbQ1tRbyfOD/

- Diaspora colmado copy: “El Colmado no es solo para los bachateros… los salseros también tienen casa aquí.” Harlem, not SD, but the room name is the same.  
  https://www.instagram.com/reel/DUBWuI8DCkq/

### What official sources do **not** support

The lock is a **room split**, not a claim that colmados play only salsa + merengue.

- UNESCO inscribed Dominican **bachata** in 2019 as “omnipresent in community celebrations and social gatherings.”  
  https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/music-and-dance-of-dominican-bachata-01514

- Same knycx page that describes the colmado speaker treats merengue **and** bachata as the national pair.  
  https://knycxjourneying.com/dominican-rhythms-how-merengue-and-bachata-define-a-nation/

- North-coast real-estate blog (tertiary): old-school bachata “still playing in colmados.”  
  https://c21perdomo.com/blog/old-school-bachata-dominican-republic

- Field clip: Elvis Martínez bachata playing in a Zona Colonial colmado.  
  https://www.tiktok.com/@carolinarosa_official/video/7146746336681987334

**Hold rule:** salsa dura + merengue de fiesta is the correct **esquina doorway** (hips + shout in the first 15 seconds). Bachata on that speaker is real and is already owned by **Silla**. Dembow is real and is gated. Do not rewrite the IN list to “whatever a colmado plays.”

## 2. Son by Four — *A Puro Dolor* is pop-ballad / Puerto Rican, not the opener

**Lock text:** OUT of Esquina AYER as opener. Cautionary tale. Wrong room (salsa-romántica ballad). 2000 = AYER time, still refuse the door.

### Identity

- Wikipedia: Puerto Rican band, formed in Puerto Rico in the late 1990s by Omar Alfanno. Genres listed: **Latin pop, salsa**. Best known for *A Puro Dolor* and the English *Purest of Pain*.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_by_Four

- Same page: “The song was released in ballad and tropical versions”; the ballad ran on Mexican/Venezuelan telenovelas; English version reached Billboard Hot 100 #26; they later moved to Catholic music.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_by_Four

- Song page: Puerto Rican band; written by Omar Alfanno; two album versions, **salsa and ballad**; ballad arranged by Alejandro Jaén; genres **Latin pop + salsa**.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Puro_Dolor

- Encyclopedia.com (Contemporary Musicians, 2004 — older than two years): 20 weeks #1 Hot Latin Tracks; “tropical/salsa group”; “sometimes described as a Latin boy band”; ballad version on the soap *La Vida en el Espejo*; English *The Purest of Pain*. One line says “origins in Orlando, Florida” (Sony/Miami market), which conflicts with Wikipedia’s Puerto Rico formation. **Prefer Wikipedia + Billboard for origin: Puerto Rican act, U.S. Latin-pop campaign.**  
  https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/son-four

### Pop-ballad, not salsa dura

- Billboard’s own 21st-century Latin-pop canon, Leila Cobo: **“Who knew a boy band could be so poignant?”** Credits the **ballad version** (“even more so, from its ballad version, which showcased López’s voice with piercing pathos”).  
  https://www.billboard.com/lists/best-latin-pop-songs-21st-century/

- Billboard 21st-century Hot Latin Songs recap: “brew of **tropical and nostalgic pop**”; 20 weeks #1 beginning April 1, 2000.  
  https://www.billboard.com/lists/top-hot-latin-songs-of-21st-century-chart-10-biggest-hits

- Billboard Latin Music Awards 2001 coverage: *A Puro Dolor* won **Hot Latin track of the year, pop track of the year, and tropical/salsa track of the year**. Dual win is the trap — tropical tags will promote it; pop/ballad is how Billboard’s editors remember it.  
  https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/son-by-four-wins-big-at-billboard-latin-awards-79959

- Billboard Latin Pop Airplay longest #1s: “A quintessential 2000s anthem,” 19 weeks atop Latin Pop Airplay.  
  https://www.billboard.com/lists/latin-pop-airplay-longest-leading-no-1s

- Apple Music artist page lists **“A Puro Dolor (Balada)”** as a primary title.  
  https://music.apple.com/us/artist/son-by-4/110940318

- Official English video, Sony channel: *Purest of Pain (A Puro Dolor)*.  
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YUGUySygZuU

- Official Spanish clip used in the local scrape: `kAKVT1HWNsg`.  
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kAKVT1HWNsg

- Spotify still ships a separate “A Puro Dolor - Salsa Version.” That cut is why a tropical-tag sort puts this first. It is not the version that made the boy-band / telenovela / Latin-pop record.  
  https://open.spotify.com/track/6M03z3sBED3z2dVJgt7F7j

**Hold rule:** 746M views do not move it onto the colmado door. Year 2000 is AYER. Room is dryer/wedding/TV-pop, not shout/tumbao.

## 4. *La Gozadera* is not the esquina door

**Lock text:** HOY OUT as first. “Next Son by Four.” Cuban TV carnival. Never first. Optional after #10 only if Javy wants one party-salsa now-cut.

### Identity and year

- Wikipedia song page: Cuban duo **Gente de Zona** feat. **Marc Anthony**; lead single from *Visualízate*; released **30 April 2015**; **genre = Latin pop**; Motiff production; a **salsa version** also exists, produced by Sergio George; video directed by Alejandro Pérez, **filmed in Cuba**, Marc Anthony scenes **filmed in the Dominican Republic**; official Copa América 2021 song; **>1.6B YouTube views as of May 2025**.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Gozadera

- Wikipedia artist page: **“Cuban reggaeton band”** from Alamar, Havana; *La Gozadera* “described by many as ‘Latin America’s official hymn.’”  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gente_de_Zona

### Why it fails the doorway

- Billboard (Leila Cobo era piece, 2015): Marc Anthony “teamed up with **Cuban reggaeton duo Gente de Zona** for an **all-ages track** accompanied by a **euphoric dancing-in-the-streets clip shot mainly in Havana**.” “The video is as much a **promo for Cuba** as it is the song… it will teach everyone some **easy Cuban salsa moves**.” “Following a tropical music lyric tradition, *La Gozadera* **calls out a list of countries** from the Spanish-speaking world.”  
  https://www.billboard.com/music/latin/marc-anthony-gente-de-zona-team-up-in-havana-for-la-gozadera-video-6590512/

- Official video `VMp55KH_3wo`, GenteDeZonaVEVO, 5 June 2015. This is the 1.7B-class clip the HOY table refuses.  
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMp55KH_3wo

- Spotify also ships “La Gozadera (feat. Marc Anthony) - **Salsa Version**.” Same trap as Son by Four’s salsa mix: a tropical tag will sort it first.  
  https://open.spotify.com/track/6idHa0JoytQc5vCgqU4NUE

- Latin Times (2015): Havana + Santo Domingo street montage, 100+ dancers, Cuban TV-ballet choreographer Roclan. Party clip, not a colmado piano.  
  https://www.latintimes.com/gente-de-zona-la-gozadera-feat-marc-anthony-when-where-watch-new-music-video-320513

- Singles Jukebox: “Cuban reggaeton duo… Party Anthems 101.”  
  https://thesinglesjukebox.com/gente-de-zona-ft-marc-anthony-la-gozadera/

Charts (Wikipedia, citing Billboard): Hot Latin Songs #2, Latin Airplay #1, **Latin Rhythm Airplay #1**, Tropical Airplay #1, Spain #1. Rhythm + Tropical dual leadership is the *Bailando* formula, not Lavoe / Rosario / Yiyo.

**Hold rule:** 2015 is HOY time. Room is wrong (Cuban / pan-Latin TV carnival). Views (1.75B local scrape vs Yiyo 357M) are the same failure mode as Son by Four. Do not open. Do not use the Sergio George salsa version as a loophole.
