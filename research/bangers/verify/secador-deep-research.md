**All four product rules hold.** The sources do not invent a musicological category called “salon-dryer music.” They do lock **temperature and place**: sit-down weep (balada, bolero, merengue *with* a grief lyric) versus dance-floor / street / men’s-barrio guitar versus 2010s women-radio romántico versus high-BPM street-club urbano.

## Key findings

### 1. Balada / bolero / merengue sentimental belong under a salon dryer — CONFIRMED (PARTIAL on the merengue label)

**Answer:** Sit-down weep is the documented job of Latin balada and bolero. Juan Gabriel *Amor Eterno* is the funeral/mamá monument. José José is the named prince of the romantic ballad. Bolero is Smithsonian-tagged as love gained / lost / ruined. Olga Tañón *Como Olvidar* (2001) is the right *lyric* for the dryer (rain, coffee, how to forget) even though the merengue cut still moves.

**Evidence**

- NPR (2019): Juan Gabriel “wrote a ballad that would become the most-played song at memorials and funerals in his home country.” The lyric was “inspired by the loss of his mother.” Ranchera/canción is used “to express our grief and our joys.” Martha Gonzalez on first-line recognition: “people will start crying before they even hear the lyrics.”  
  Sources: https://www.npr.org/2019/08/11/749901083/juan-gabriels-amor-eterno-takes-on-new-meaning-after-el-paso-shooting · https://www.npr.org/transcripts/749901083
- NPR Alt.Latino (2019): same song, written after his mother died; life of its own at memorials.  
  Source: https://www.npr.org/sections/altlatino/2019/08/08/749358299/amor-eterno-for-el-paso-gilroy-dayton-and-beyond
- Official monument the room already uses: Juan Gabriel — *Amor Eterno (En Vivo Desde el Instituto Nacional de Bellas Artes)*.  
  Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RgKqxLAhRKE
- AllMusic on José José: “El Príncipe de la Canción”; *El Triste* (1970 festival) made him a star; catalog is rancheras, boleros, pop ballads. Moods include laid-back/mellow, romantic, soothing. Compilations are literally titled *Idolos de la Balada*, *Grandes de la Balada*, *Las 50 Grandes Canciones de la Balada en Español*.  
  Source: https://www.allmusic.com/artist/jos%C3%A9-jos%C3%A9-mn0000822874
- Apple Music: “José José is a master of romantic pop balladry… yearning inflections.”  
  Source: https://music.apple.com/us/artist/jos%C3%A9-jos%C3%A9/333303
- Smithsonian Folkways on bolero (Chelo Silva): “sultry and emotive recordings imparted a certain grace to the whole notion of love gained, love lost, and love ruined.”  
  Source: https://folkways.si.edu/chelo-silva/la-reina-tejana-del-bolero/latin-world/music/album/smithsonian
- Billboard (2021): Olga Tañón *Como Olvidar* (album *Yo Por Ti*, 2001) “peaked at No. 1 on the Hot Latin Songs chart dated Sept. 22, 2001.”  
  Source: https://www.billboard.com/music/latin/olga-tanon-como-olvidar-comparison-jay-wheeler-9524257/
- AllMusic: *Yo Por Ti* won a Grammy for Best Merengue Performance; “the album did manage a number one hit, ‘Como Olvidar.’” Mood tags on Olga include **Sentimental** *and* Energetic / Fun. She is merengue + Latin pop, not a ranchera funeral singer.  
  Source: https://www.allmusic.com/artist/olga-ta%C3%B1%C3%B3n-mn0000466442
- Warner official video copy still sells the merengue cut as “vibrant merengue rhythms” / “ideal para bailar.” Lyric, not tambora, is what seats it under the hood: “Viendo la lluvia caer / Sola tomando café.”  
  Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OkOJGgqcwro · https://genius.com/Olga-tanon-como-olvidar-merengue-version-lyrics · https://open.spotify.com/intl-es/track/7gT6BKjzmmZM0FkYJjPSqv
- Vernacular salon practice (not scholarly): peluquería playlists and clips pair Juan Gabriel / boleros-baladas with the chair. Useful as folk confirmation, weak as a citation.  
  Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q0pXDboelHI · https://www.tiktok.com/@claudiamelenica/video/7573101624428629256

**What this does *not* prove:** No UNESCO / Grove / Smithsonian page says “this is music for a hood dryer.” The product lock is temperature (lump in the throat, alone-with-the-song). Balada and bolero match that job. *Merengue sentimental* is the room’s name for Olga/Milly weep-in-merengue-clothes, not a published subgenre with a Grove entry.

### 2. Merengue de fiesta and bachata típica do NOT belong under the dryer — CONFIRMED

**Answer:** Merengue’s official identity is dance, festival, pair-work, tambora. *Suavemente* and *El Africano* are dance-floor staples. Bachata típica is guitar-barrio / men’s-bar music (Pacini Hernández; iASO). That is Silla and Esquina/Marquesina, not the hood.

**Merengue de fiesta**

- UNESCO ICH (inscribed 2016): merengue is national identity; “social gatherings and celebrations”; “danced in pairs”; festivals in Santo Domingo and Puerto Plata; National Merengue Day.  
  Source: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/music-and-dance-of-the-merengue-in-the-dominican-republic-01162
- Smithsonian Folkways (*Merengue Típico from the Dominican Republic*, La India Canela): “driving dance beat, aggressive improvisations, and down-home lyrics.” Accordion + tambora + güira.  
  Source: https://folkways.si.edu/la-india-canela/merengue-tipico-from-the-dominican-republic/caribbean-latin-world/music/album/smithsonian
- iASO history: merengue is “a Caribbean dance and music style in 2/4 time,” national music of the DR; perico ripiao named for a brothel; Trujillo moved it into “respectable ballrooms”; típico second section is “hard-driving mambo” to “stimulate dancers.”  
  Source: https://www.iasorecords.com/music/a-history-of-merengue-popular-music-of-the-dominican-republic-perico-ripiao-merengue-tipico
- Paul Austerlitz on Afropop (2006): the merengue aesthetic is *subido* — “euphoria and exuberance”; danced in ballroom position. That is hips-up, not hood-down.  
  Source: https://www.afropop.org/articles/10658
- Apple Music on Elvis Crespo *Suavemente* (1998): “It’s a nonstop dance party from there… hyperactive horns, frenetic keys, and percussive tambora and guiro.”  
  Source: https://music.apple.com/us/album/suavemente/187429585
- Billboard (2018): *Suavemente* “still gets fans out of their seats and onto the dance floor.”  
  Source: https://www.billboard.com/music/latin/elvis-crespo-suavemente-20th-anniversary-8317952/
- Wikipedia (secondary, citing Billboard): title song became “a staple in Latin-music nightclubs”; “staple at any Latino gathering” that “elicits attendees to dance.” Album was the first merengue set to top Top Latin Albums.  
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suavemente
- Wilfrido Vargas *El Africano* (1983/84, album *El Funcionario*): international merengue hit used as the lock’s “not this” example. Dance-floor staple, not a weep.  
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfrido_Vargas · https://www.allmusic.com/album/el-africano-mw0001077528

**Bachata típica**

- Deborah Pacini Hernández, Harvard *ReVista* (2016), restating *Bachata: A Social History of a Dominican Popular Music* (Temple, 1995): after Trujillo, rural musicians settle in Santo Domingo shantytowns; guitar-based *música popular*; the word *bachata* first meant a backyard party, then a slur for “vulgar and uncouth.” Urbanized sound = “shantytown life, especially the social and emotional disruptions experienced by **underemployed men**.” Live shows in “neighborhood bars that ‘respectable’ folk would consider disreputable.” Only Radio Guarachita would play it. Luis Vargas and Raulín Rodríguez are the 1990s generation that walked through the door JLG opened.  
  Source: https://revista.drclas.harvard.edu/dominican-bachata/
- Book itself: Temple University Press, 1995; Internet Archive scan.  
  Sources: https://books.google.com/books/about/Bachata.html?id=oLdkCugOAOcC · https://archive.org/details/bachatasocialhis00paci
- CUNY Dominican Studies Institute: Pacini Hernández is “the first scholarly study of bachata, a guitar-based dance music from the Dominican Republic.”  
  Source: https://www.ccny.cuny.edu/dsi/deborah-pacini-hernandez-bachata-music-collection
- iASO on Antony Santos / Luis Vargas / Raulín Rodríguez: modern típica begins with Blas Durán’s electric guitar (1987); Santos/Vargas/Raulín “defining what the music has come to sound like”; *Voy pa’llá* (1991) is romantic but the guitar language is Mayimbe/thumb-pick, merengue-weighted, built for people who “didn’t know how to dance” the old stigma bachata. That is shop-guitar, not hood-piano.  
  Source: https://www.iasorecords.com/artists/antony-santos-bachata?page=255%2C1

### 4. Dembow is never dryer — CONFIRMED

**Answer:** Dembow is street / club / high-BPM urbano. Radio in the DR historically refused it. El Alfa’s own line is the placement: television and radio said no; “you step in the streets, and all you could hear was dembow.” Rolling Stone clocks the instrumentals past 135 BPM. That is hips and block, not a lump in the throat under a hood.

**Evidence**

- Billboard timeline (2022): “playful, rapid drum pattern and carefree energy”; “began as a musical pariah.” El Alfa: “When I’d go to the television and radio stations, they’d say, ‘No, we can’t play that.’ But you step in the streets, and all you could hear was dembow.” Early DJ Boyo was “met with disdain from both radio DJs who mainly played **salsa, bachata and merengue**.” 2010s names: El Alfa, Chimbala, Tokischa, Rochy RD.  
  Source: https://www.billboard.com/music/latin/hispanic-heritage-month-dembow-timeline-latin-urban-music-1235155161/
- Billboard feature: El Alfa / Chimbala / Tokischa on the once-banned genre going global.  
  Source: https://www.billboard.com/music/features/el-alfa-chimbala-tokischa-dembow-1235050035/
- Rolling Stone (2019): tracks “hurtle along”; “blistering instrumentals that can push past **135 beats per minute**.” “In order for dembow to work in the average **club**, it had to be fairly fast.” “The essence of dembow is to promote yourself in the **street**.” Radio promotion “runs contrary to the genre’s core principles.” Tracks have been banned from DR airwaves.  
  Source: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-latin/the-rise-of-dembow-el-alfa-lirico-en-la-casa-907678/
- Berklee (2024): heard “in the clubs,” TikTok, Zumba, malls; riddim from Shabba Ranks *Dem Bow* (1990) / Steely & Clevie *Fish Market*; Panama Spanish reggae → PR reggaetón → harder/faster Dominican dembow. El Alfa + Peso Pluma listed as Dominican dembow.  
  Source: https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-now/news/what-is-dembow-tracing-the-roots-of-a-global-phenomenon
- Remezcla: “faster, younger, sibling genre of reggaeton… uniquely Dominican—built on a lightning-fast percussive style.”  
  Source: https://remezcla.com/lists/music/best-dembow-of-2021/
- *Pepas* (Farruko, 24 Jun 2021) is **not** even Dominican dembow: Billboard No. 1 Hot Dance/Electronic Songs; Rolling Stone: “thunderous, amped-up EDM-and-guaracha,” “everyone on pills at the club.” Still never dryer. The lock is right to park it with El Alfa / Tokischa / Rochy as “club / never this room.”  
  Sources: https://www.billboard.com/pro/farruko-pepas-tops-hot-dance-electronic-songs-chart/ · https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-latin/farruko-pepas-god-new-music-1234594021/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepas

Dembow and *Pepas* fail the Secador test on every axis: BPM, lyric, room, radio history.

## Contrarian risks

These do **not** overturn the lock. They are the places a later pass could get sloppy.

1. **“Salón merengue” ≠ beauty salon.** Dance histories call merengue de orquesta “salon merengue” meaning *ballroom*. Do not cite that phrase as dryer evidence.  
   Source of the trap: https://www.salsavida.com/dancepedia/merengue-dance/

2. **Real peluquerías play mixed radio.** TikTok and background-music vendors sell “música para peluquería” that can be lounge, pop, or dance. The lock is authored temperature, not a census of salon speakers.

3. **Olga *Como Olvidar* is still merengue.** Warner and AllMusic keep the dance tag. A Marquesina DJ can play it. Protect it in Secador on **lyric and tía-fame**, not by pretending the tambora disappeared. *Es Mentiroso* has the same dual passport.

4. **Típica can weep.** *Voy pa’llá* is romantic. Pacini Hernández also records a romantic-lyric line (Luis Segura) next to bawdy bar songs. The OUT rule is **place** (barrio bar / barber guitar), not “típica has no tears.” Do not let a weep-lyric típica leak into the dryer 15.

5. **JLG *Burbujas* / *Bachata Rosa* will keep leaking.** Pacini Hernández: *Bachata Rosa* (1990) is the middle-class stamp of approval, “highly stylized, elegant.” That is couple-radio (Love Birds gap in `GENRE-LOCK.md`), not tía-funeral and not Mayimbe típica. Keep it unsowned.

6. **Romeo 2013 already feels “classic” to some tías.** *Propuesta* is HOY by date, AYER-adjacent by ritual. Date lock still wins. Do not park it on AYER because a 45-year-old already knows the violin.

7. ***Deja Vu* is dance-coded in the video.** Billboard’s coverage is Shakira *practicing bachata*. Temperature in the room is still dryer-romántico (Shakira as tía-radio), not Marquesina hips. If a later cut leans club, drop it.

8. ***Pepas* is guaracha-EDM, not Dominican dembow.** The ban still holds. Just don’t write “Farruko is dembow” in the lock copy.

9. **AYER window vs song birth.** José José *El Triste* is 1970; Camilo Sesto *Vivir Así…* is 1978. The lock already admits them as fame monuments inside an 80s–2010 *radio* window. Do not use that courtesy for 2011–now catalog.

10. **Agent and scrape agree; no conflict to resolve.** Job `01a00b58-5916-71a3-bbaa-e81b9ad800ff` returned the same four verdicts. Its extra sources (Austerlitz/Afropop, AllMusic bolero style page, LA Times on *Pepas* as Latin house) strengthen claims 2 and 4. It did not find a scholarly salon-dryer ethnography either.
